The Wave Spectrum: one family, pico to exa.

One family of industrial models on a single SI scale - from a board beside the machine to an exascale-class flagship. They are built from scratch to read machines and to talk to the people who run them, and the same family fits a hobbyist’s single-board computer and a plant datacenter. Each tier has a different job, and a measurement decides which job it is allowed to take.

§1 / SCALE

Sized for where it has to live.

A tier is not a rank. Each one is cut for the job it does and the hardware that job happens on: Wave Pico fits a single-board computer bolted next to one machine, Wave Exa fills a datacenter. The rail puts every tier at the size its artifact really is, so the distance between two of them is a real distance.

§2 / CAPABILITY

How much of a big model fits in a small one.

Many large models are built out of hundreds of small specialist parts, and only a few of them fire on any one word. So we took a frontier model, kept 60 of its 896 parts, threw the rest away, and gave what was left the plant’s real work. What comes through the cut is the question this page is about.

The line, not the damage. Structure came through the cut. Explanation did not. That line is the useful part of the experiment: extraction, classification and tool use keep working when a model is squeezed down to something you can put beside a machine, and explaining or reasoning at length needs the room upstairs. So the trick behind a small industrial model is not to shrink a big one and hope. It is to find the part of a big model’s competence that survives being small, and build a small model that is all of that part.

The family is built along the line. Wave Pico and Wave Nano are trained from scratch for the work that survives: read a machine, say what it is doing, say how sure you are. Wave Micro and above carry the reasoning and the explaining. A job goes to a slot by which side of that line it falls on, not by how impressive the model sounds.

WHAT WE HAVE MEASURED OURSELVES

Nothing on our industrial bench is a conversation. A window of raw telemetry goes in; one word from a locked list comes out, with a margin beside it saying how sure the model is. The 30B-class open models we have put on that bench read raw telemetry near chance. No frontier or chat-tuned model has ever been measured there, so that is the whole of the claim. A task-native model orders of magnitude smaller does that reading as its ordinary job.

Specialists first. Wave Pico and Wave Nano are task-native total specialists. By design they score at chance on general benchmarks (MMLU 26.9 against a 25 chance line, Pico v4 audit). Reasoning and explanation grow from Wave Micro upward, where every tier must stay competitive on general benchmarks as well as industrial ones.

Built to read machines. The input side is machine-native: a window of raw telemetry, a log line, a serial dump - the thing a machine emits, not a sentence about it. That is what training from scratch buys. The vocabulary is the plant’s rather than the web’s, and a tier can read the output of the tier beneath it.

How the recipes split. Scratch means trained from random init on our data, and it is bounded by diverse tokens, not GPUs - measured healthy with no starvation knee through 1.5 billion parameters. Pico and Nano are the scratch tiers. From Micro up, base + specialize: a strong open base, industrial continued-pretrain, and the mesh. Peta and Exa are carved from the flagship rather than trained. Sizes are targets and bands, not final specifications.

The microcontroller is the sensor, not the model. An ESP32 or an Arduino is what reads the vibration, the temperature or the current and hands it up. No Wave tier runs on one: Wave Pico’s weights are tens of megabytes quantized, and a microcontroller has megabytes of flash. The smallest thing that runs a Wave tier is a Pi-class board with no GPU. Roger Edge, a classifier of a few hundred kilobytes, is what lives down on the node.

Run it where you like. Every tier is a model you host - on the board beside the machine, on a server in the plant, or on your own GPU at home. A model you run locally can also go on air as a band with roger share, which is how anything on RogerAI is reached remotely. Local or remote is a deployment choice, not a different product.

One family on one wire. Every tier ships with Wave Mesh baked in and understands the output of every tier beneath it.

§6 / WAVE INFINITE

The model that grows.

Prototype · measured foundations, growth loop in development

Every slot above is a fixed size. Wave Infinite is a prototype model program. Start from a Nano, Micro, or Giga base, and the model grows new weights: real parameters, trained on your work, not prompts and not retrieval. All of it happens inside a proof that the base behavior is preserved.

TRAINING IT IS USING IT
  1. Start. Pick a base: Nano, Micro, or Giga. Infinite is the loop, not another tier.
  2. Work. The model observes its own execution and grows new weights only where measured utility says they help.
  3. Certify. Changes go live through the same certified path, with an exact rollback always resident. If the work shifts beyond what is certified, it reverts to the original path by itself.
WHAT WE HAVE PROVEN
The certificate is sound
Removing every expert it marked unreachable left the model’s routing bit-identical - not close, unchanged.
Specialization is safe on stable work
Within a single domain the guard almost never fires, so the specialization holds for a stable workload.
The guard is necessary
The moment the workload changes domain, the same certificates would miss a large share of decisions. The fallback is what makes aggression safe.
Standard pruning over-removes
Frequency analysis nominated an order of magnitude more for removal than could be proven safe, across three model architectures.

Measured on interim hardware; figures publish after final-hardware re-verification.

IN MEASUREMENT

End-to-end speed of certified specialization, and the growth layer’s quality gains. The prototype program publishes both with error bars.

What it requires. The certificate is reachability certification of mixture-of-experts routing. It applies only to a model that has experts and a per-expert selection bias to read. That is a base-model choice rather than something the family has today. It does not reach the microcontroller-class edge at all: a classifier of a few hundred kilobytes is not a language model.
