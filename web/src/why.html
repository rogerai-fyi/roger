You could go direct. Here is what you would be giving up.

Every commercial AI provider will happily sell you tokens without us in the middle, and an aggregator will front a hundred of them for a few percent. So the 10% routing fee on a curated band deserves a straight answer: what does it buy, what does it not buy, and when should you skip us entirely. This page is that answer, including the last part.

§1 / IDENTITY UNLINKING

The provider sees a station. It never sees you.

When you use a commercial API directly, the provider holds the whole picture: your account, your card, your IP, your request times, and every prompt, joined together under one customer record. When you tune a curated band here, the request leaves for that provider from a station's own account. The provider sees one caller making many requests. It cannot tell our consumers apart, cannot join your requests into a profile, and never learns a name, wallet, or billing identity - none of that is on the wire. There are no tracking headers added in transit; what the station forwards is your request body and nothing about who you are.

what this does not buy

Your prompt content still reaches whoever operates the model - that is what answering it means. Identity unlinking means the provider cannot tie the words to you; it does not mean the words are private. If the content itself must not leave machines you trust, use a confidential station (hardware-attested) or run your own Tower and keep the whole loop on your own network. We would rather tell you this here than have you find it out later.

§2 / THE ROUTING

One dial, and the request always lands somewhere.

The band you tune is not one machine. It is every station serving that model - people's GPUs and curated commercial relays, counted and labeled apart - under one endpoint that does not change. The broker picks by measured capacity and health, retries a failed station on the next one mid-request, and holds every request to the price cap you set: your maximum rides each call, so nobody can raise a rate under you. When a home GPU serves you for less, you pay less; when it drops off the air at 2am, the answer still arrives. You never manage a provider account, an API key per vendor, or a failover config. One wallet, one key, per-token billing, spend limits and a monthly budget you set yourself.

And every answer comes with a receipt the station signed and the broker counter-signed, naming who served it, what it cost, and how the price was derived. On a curated band your history shows the split itself: the provider's list price and the routing fee, side by side. A bill you can verify is rarer than it should be.

The routing fee is for everyone whose requests are worth unlinking, whose bills are worth verifying, and whose uptime is worth more than one vendor's status page.

§4 / YOUR OWN TOWER

The exit is part of the product.

A standalone Tower is the same routing, on your hardware, for your people, for free. It admits your own stations, serves your own network, issues local receipts, and never bills anyone - there is no account and nothing that phones home; the isolation is enforced in code, not promised in copy. Point it at your local models, or attach a curated relay with your own provider key and give your whole office one clean endpoint in front of a commercial API. Private bands on the public network sit between the two: hidden from the market, reachable only by a frequency code you hold.

We build it this way on purpose. A network you can leave without asking permission is the only kind worth trusting with your traffic, and the reader who could self-host and chose not to is the customer we actually want.
