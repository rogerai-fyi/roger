((•)) THE TOWER

Every station dials the tower. The tower never dials a station.

One endpoint in front of machines nobody controls centrally. The tower decides which station takes your request, counts what it actually used, and co-signs a receipt that neither side can forge alone. In the API, the manual and the source it is called the broker - same thing, older name.

§1 / THE PATCH

Choosing a station is a score, not a queue.

Every station on air is scored for your request, and the score is divided by how much work that station is already carrying.

§2 / THE TAPE

A station cannot profit from misreporting the bill.

Every served request produces a receipt signed by the station that served it and counter-signed by the broker, chained to that station’s previous one.

The price is not the station’s to claim. A station’s claimed price is discarded at settlement and the broker’s own resolved price is used, so quoting one number and billing another is not a thing that can happen.

The token count is checked, not trusted. The broker recounts the tokens itself and bills the lower of the claim and its own recount, on the prompt and the completion separately.

The ceiling is set before the work starts. Cost is capped at the hold authorised up front, so a request cannot bill past what you agreed to when it began.

What happens when it goes wrong is the guarantee. A receipt signed with the wrong key does not verify, so it settles nothing and the hold is refunded in full. A request that returns no usable output costs zero, and the hold comes back. If settlement itself fails, the hold is refunded - the failure path leans toward the caller, not the house.

§3 / WHAT IT IS NOT

The honest edges.

The tower relays your request to the station that serves it, so it handles the request and the response in transit. What we retain, and for how long, is in the privacy policy - not implied away here. It is a routing, metering and settlement service, and everything above describes what it does with a request, not a claim about who else could see one.
