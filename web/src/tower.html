((•)) THE TOWER

Every station dials the tower. The tower never dials a station.

One endpoint in front of machines nobody controls centrally. The tower decides which station takes your request, counts what it actually used, and co-signs a receipt that neither side can forge alone. In the API, the manual and the source it is called the broker - same thing, older name.

§3 / THE TAPE

A station cannot profit from misreporting the bill.

Every served request produces a receipt signed by the station that served it and counter-signed by the broker, chained to that station’s previous one.

The price is not the station’s to claim. A station’s claimed price is discarded at settlement and the broker’s own resolved price is used, so quoting one number and billing another is not a thing that can happen.

The token count is checked, not trusted. The broker recounts the tokens itself and bills the lower of the claim and its own recount, on the prompt and the completion separately.

The ceiling is set before the work starts. Cost is capped at the hold authorized up front, so a request cannot bill past what you agreed to when it began.

What happens when it goes wrong is the guarantee. A receipt signed with the wrong key does not verify, so it settles nothing and the hold is refunded in full. A request that returns no usable output costs zero, and the hold comes back. If settlement itself fails, the hold is refunded - the failure path leans toward the caller, not the house.

§4 / RUN ONE

The tower is a program. You can run one.

A Tower carries work between the people asking and the machines answering. It runs no model of its own, and it is paid for the carrying: every settled request pays the serving node 70% of that node’s own listed price, the tower operator 10%, and the platform the remaining 20%. What a Tower can and cannot see is the same as everything above on this page - the runbook is exact about it.

Linux only, and on purpose: a Tower is a long-running server process. The installer verifies the release checksum before it installs anything, and refuses outright on macOS or Windows rather than leaving you a binary that cannot serve.

What your Tower carries and earns shows up on your payouts page, beside anything you earn from serving, and cashes out the same way. Traffic through any one Tower is early, so expect that figure to start at zero and follow demand rather than the day you install.

Or join nothing at all. A Tower does not have to be part of this network. In standalone mode it needs no account and makes no outbound connection: it mints its own local network and trust root, admits the machines you invite, and routes between them. That is a private relay for your own local models – a household, a lab, an air-gapped site – with nothing leaving your machines and nobody to sign up with.

Standalone routing is free and locally accounted. Every receipt names your local network and claims nothing about RogerAI: there is no credit, no payout and no platform share, and the configuration refuses a payout setting rather than quietly defaulting it off. It is not an open relay either – only the client you admitted may route through it. The runbook has the commands, and two commented example configurations ship in the repository under packaging/tower/.

§5 / WHAT YOU GET BACK

The receipt leaves with the work.

Every response carries its co-signed receipt on the X-RogerAI-Receipt header, so the evidence arrives in your hands rather than living only in our database. It carries the model, the token counts both sides settled on, the price that was actually used, and both signatures. The same records show up on your usage page, and the station’s signature is verified before anything settles - that check runs on every request, not at audit time.
