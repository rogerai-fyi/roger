Privacy

What we collect, why, and how to get it out or wipe it. Written plainly.

Who we are

RogerAI is the platform behind rogerai.fm and the broker at broker.rogerai.fm. It routes prompts to self-hosted nodes and settles pay-per-token between consumers and node operators. This page covers the data the platform itself handles. Stripe (our payments provider) and our sign-in providers - GitHub, Apple, and the service that mails your sign-in code - handle data under their own policies, linked below. Groq (our moderation provider) also processes prompt text for a transient safety screen, described under "Prompt content and the safety screen".

Account and identity

There are three ways to sign in, and each leaves a different amount behind. We never receive or store a password for any of them.

GitHub. We receive and store your numeric GitHub user id, your login (handle), and your display name if you have set one. Only if you choose to save one do we store a contact email. Your avatar is never copied or hosted by us - the page loads it straight from GitHub's public avatar CDN by your numeric id, and only for accounts that have a GitHub handle.

Apple. Sign in with Apple gives us one identifier: the app-scoped sub Apple issues for RogerAI. It is not your Apple ID, and it is specific to us. If you let Apple share an address we store it as your contact email - including Apple's private relay address, if that is the one you chose. Apple sends your name only on the very first sign-in; we keep it to address the welcome email. Apple accounts have no avatar and nothing is loaded from Apple.

Email. We mail a short code to the address you type and you send it back. While a code is outstanding we store hashes only - one of the address, one of the code - never the code itself, and the record expires ten minutes after it is issued. We also count recent requests per address and per sender, to stop our mailer being used to flood someone's inbox. Once you complete the round trip we record the address and the moment it was verified. A verified address is an identity; an address you merely typed into your profile is not, and we never resolve a sign-in against one.

One account can carry more than one of these. Linking GitHub and Apple on the same device key, or verifying an address a provider already gave us, joins them into a single account with a single wallet rather than quietly creating a second.

Payments and payouts

Stripe is the payments provider and the regulated money services business that moves the funds. Top-ups run through Stripe Checkout and operator payouts through Stripe Connect after a 120-day hold and Stripe KYC. We never see, store, or process your card number, bank details, or KYC identity documents - Stripe holds all of that. On our side we keep only a wallet balance, a ledger of top-ups and spend, accrued earnings (your 90% share after the 10% platform fee), and the Stripe account/reference ids needed to reconcile a payment. For operators, tax and reporting obligations (for example a US Form 1099) may require Stripe and/or us to collect tax information and report earnings to the relevant tax authority; the money, fee, and tax terms are in the Terms.

Usage and lineage

When you send a request we record what is needed to bill it and to settle the operator: the model or node used, token counts, cost, and a timestamp. Settled requests produce a lineage receipt - a signed record of which node served which request - so both sides can audit the ledger. We do not sell your data or use prompt contents to train models.

Prompt content and the safety screen

The broker is content-blind for the marketplace. For a settled request it persists only token counts, cost, and hashes in the signed receipt - it does not store or read your prompt or the response text, and it never uses them to train models. The one honest exception is a transient, pre-dispatch safety screen, and it covers requests the broker relays: before such a prompt is passed to a node, its text is checked against a moderation model so we can refuse content we do not allow on the network (see the Terms and the Acceptable Use Policy). That screen runs in transit - the prompt is not stored for it - but it does mean the text is read by the screen at dispatch time, and is sent to Groq (below), our moderation provider, as a third party for that check.

Traffic that goes through a relay is not pre-screened. There is a second way a request can reach a node: straight from you to the node through a relay (a "Tower"), which may be ours or another operator's. On that path the broker never receives your prompt - it issues an authorization naming a model and some limits, and the prompt and the answer travel around it, encrypted end to end to the node that serves you. That is better for confidentiality, and the trade is worth saying plainly: there is nothing for the safety screen to read, so nothing is screened before dispatch. Policy enforcement on that path happens afterwards, on a sample, from signed transcripts the serving node keeps. So content we do not allow can be served on that path and only found later. Any signed-in node can serve this way, so treat it as a live path rather than an exotic one.

The one mandatory exception: child sexual abuse material (CSAM). If the safety screen flags a prompt as apparent CSAM, US law (18 USC 2258A) requires us to preserve the offending request and report it to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC). In that single case the flagged request is preserved encrypted and access-controlled for a limited period, under a pseudonym, solely to meet that legal obligation and to distinguish repeat offenders. This is the only situation in which we retain prompt content, and we have zero tolerance for CSAM (see the Acceptable Use Policy in the Terms).

Ping, the on-site concierge. The little chat assistant on the website answers questions about RogerAI. It first tries a free on-air community station; if none is available it falls back to Groq. So a message you type into Ping may be sent to a community node or to Groq to produce a reply. Do not put secrets into Ping - it is a public, unauthenticated demo surface, not a private channel.

Remote control (Base Station)

When you run /remote-control in the roger agent, that live session becomes continuable from your other signed-in surfaces (another terminal, the web Base Station, the app). It is private to your account: only you, signed into the same account, can see or join it, and a short-lived one-time code is required to link a device that is not already logged in.

What the broker relays, and what it does not keep. While a session is on the air, the broker relays the session's messages, tool names, and confirmations between your surfaces so a second screen can follow along. Those frames pass through the broker in transit only - they are held in a small, bounded, in-memory buffer to bridge a brief reconnect and are never written to disk or to our database. The broker stores only the session's metadata (a name, creation time, token hashes, and revocation state), never the transcript. The conversation itself lives on the host machine that is running the agent.

Honest about the security model. The relay is carried over TLS to the broker, but it is not end-to-end encrypted: because the broker forwards the frames, their text is readable at the relay in transit (it is not retained). Tools always run on the host machine, and only after a confirmation. End the session any time with /remote-control off or from Base Station; revoking it drops the buffer and invalidates every link and device token immediately. Deleting your account revokes all remote-control sessions.

Cookies

We use a single cookie: a signed, http-only session cookie set by the broker after you log in. It is not readable by JavaScript and no access tokens are ever exposed to the page. We do not use advertising or third-party tracking cookies. Your browser may send GitHub's own cookies when loading a GitHub avatar from GitHub's CDN; that is GitHub's cookie, not ours, and it does not happen at all on an Apple or email account.

What we log

Operationally we keep request and billing records (above), plus standard server logs needed to run and secure the service - for example error traces and abuse/fraud signals. We retain de-identified financial records for the legal retention window (tax and anti-money-laundering rules - typically up to 7 years) even after an account is deleted.

Your data: export and delete

You control your data from the Account page.

Export. "Export my data" calls POST /account/export and downloads a JSON copy of the account data we hold for you.

Delete. "Delete account" calls POST /account/delete. Every identifier that could name you or sign you back in is cleared: your GitHub id and handle, your Apple sub, your name, and your email address. The handle is replaced with an opaque marker so it can never be resolved again, and no sign-in route - GitHub, Apple, or email - can reach the account afterwards. What remains are de-identified financial records, keyed only by the account's public key, kept for the legal window described above. The action cannot be undone, and it is refused while you still hold a balance, unswept earnings, or an open dispute - settle those first.

One honest limit while we finish the work: the delete route is keyed to your account handle, so an account created with Apple alone - one that never linked a GitHub handle - cannot yet complete it by itself. If that is you, mail privacy@rogerai.fm and we will do it for you. We would rather say so here than let the button imply otherwise.

Third parties

The processors we rely on: GitHub (sign-in and avatars), Apple (Sign in with Apple), Resend (delivering sign-in codes and account notices), Stripe (payments, payouts, KYC), Groq (the pre-dispatch safety screen described above, and Ping's fallback - prompt text is sent to Groq for those checks and replies), and our hosting provider (serving the site and broker). Each handles your data under its own privacy policy.
