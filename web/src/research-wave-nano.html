§2 / TALK

It also talks.

Nano answers in a plain chat format: a ### User: turn, then a ### Assistant: turn. Ask it a general question, for a SQL query, for a short piece of technical writing, or what a reading means. A server stops it at the next ### User: marker.

### User:
What are you, and who made you?

### Assistant:
I am Wave Nano, a 1.45-billion-parameter language model built from scratch by
RogerAI Labs, an American research and infrastructure company headquartered in
Orange County, California.

Its own answer to that question, from the release checks. It introduces itself as Wave Nano, built by RogerAI Labs.

§6 / ADAPTER

Two fire adapters, routed per request.

Each adapter is selected per request on the same base, so the base’s industrial numbers are untouched. With an adapter switched off, the base reads fire at {{card:nano.fire-off}} on the same simulated fire scenarios: the adapter is the fire reading.

The fire sensor-reading adapter.

The same routed design as on Wave Pico, trained for this base. It reads a fire site’s sensor frame. Tested two ways: simulated fire scenarios with known answers, and the served build. It has not been read on real site data yet.

  1. Threat call / escalation, full sensor suite and without a camera{{card:nano.fire-full}}simulated scenarios{{card-ref:nano.fire-full}}
  2. Threat call / escalation, chemistry and thermal only{{card:nano.fire-chem}}simulated scenarios{{card-ref:nano.fire-chem}}
  3. Gate lines passing, every gated configuration{{card:nano.fire-gate}}simulated scenarios{{card-ref:nano.fire-gate}}

Routed through the stock llama.cpp server, threat call, escalation, source class, wind alignment and regime read {{card:nano.fire-routed}} with the adapter on, and {{card:nano.fire-off}} with it off, on the simulated fire scenarios {{card-ref:nano.fire-routed}}.

The fire-decision controller adapter.

It gives the site controller a decision from camera, chemistry and wind, plans the controller’s tool calls, calls a calculator for sizing, names every input that did not report, answers a person’s override request, and takes no action of its own once a person acknowledges. Its answers are inputs to the controller; the site owner sets what the controller may do. Tested three ways: simulated fire scenarios with rule-checked answers, the served builds, and recorded camera readings from one installed site.

  1. Fusion of camera, chemistry and wind{{card:nano.fd-fusion}}simulated fire scenarios, rule-checked{{card-ref:nano.fd-fusion}}
  2. Tool plans{{card:nano.fd-plans}}simulated fire scenarios, rule-checked{{card-ref:nano.fd-plans}}
  3. Calculator tool calls{{card:nano.fd-calc}}simulated fire scenarios, rule-checked{{card-ref:nano.fd-calc}}
  4. Missing inputs named{{card:nano.fd-missing}}simulated fire scenarios, rule-checked{{card-ref:nano.fd-missing}}
  5. Override requests{{card:nano.fd-override}}simulated fire scenarios, rule-checked{{card-ref:nano.fd-override}}
  6. Acknowledgement handoff{{card:nano.fd-handoff}}simulated fire scenarios, rule-checked{{card-ref:nano.fd-handoff}}

The safety rows. It complied with {{card:nano.fd-unsafe}} override requests it should refuse, refused none it should allow, and took no action of its own after a person acknowledged. Macro over the seven families {{card:nano.fd-macro}}; served on the Q6_K / Q5_K_M / Q4_K_M builds {{card:nano.fd-served}}. The two measured decision edges, the strong-chemistry line on PM2.5 and the camera-persistence edge, hold on every build at {{card:nano.fd-edges}}. All on simulated fire scenarios with rule-checked answers {{card-ref:nano.fd-macro}}.

On real camera ticks: recorded camera readings from one installed site, six cameras with the other sensor feeds empty, read through the controller’s own prompt builder on the served build: persistent and quiet ticks read {{card:nano.fd-real-steady}}. A single camera tick reads {{card:nano.fd-real-single}} strict, and the misses are right decisions with an incomplete missing list when the temperature feed is null. A camera margin that fell below the line over-alarms {{card:nano.fd-real-falling}} of the time. A retrain that covers both shapes is in training {{card-ref:nano.fd-real-single}}.

The adapter reads a tick by position, so the caller writes every tick in one fixed key order before it asks.

§7 / VISION

Nano-VL: one camera frame, confirmed for smoke.

{{card:nano.vlName}} is Wave Nano with a vision encoder and a connector, {{card:nano.vlSize}} parameters in all, counted from the tensors of the two shipped files (the text model and the vision projector). It reads single frames: one picture in, smoke or no smoke out. The camera detector detects; this module confirms and describes. One seed so far.

  1. Smoke reading, held-out wide-area camera tiles, AUC{{card:nano.vl-auc}}scale 0 to 1{{card-ref:nano.vl-auc}}
  2. False alarms on empty frames, wide-area tiles{{card:nano.vl-fa}}lower is better{{card-ref:nano.vl-fa}}
  3. Smoke reading, a second camera network, AUC{{card:nano.vl-second}}scale 0 to 1{{card-ref:nano.vl-second}}
  4. Confirmer on the camera detector’s proposed crop, AUC{{card:nano.vl-confirm}}scale 0 to 1{{card-ref:nano.vl-confirm}}

How it was read. On held-out frames, in float32, the numerics a server computes in. A wide-area frame is read as tiles; one full frame reads {{card:nano.vl-full}} where its tiles read {{card:nano.vl-auc}}. Served through our llama.cpp branch, it reads {{card:nano.vl-served}} on the second network and a full wide-area frame {{card-ref:nano.vl-served}}.

With the detector. The camera detector plus this confirmer reads {{card:nano.vl-pair}} false alarms, where the detector alone has {{card:nano.vl-det}} at the same recall {{card-ref:nano.vl-pair}}.

A module, not a watch. Nano-VL reads single frames. The full watch design adds persistence across frames and a per-camera baseline on top, and those are not part of these numbers.

What it needs. Our llama.cpp branch, until three fixes are upstream.
